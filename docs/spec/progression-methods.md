# Progression methods and set structures

Research note for wayfinder #27, written 2026-10-30. Where this note's proposals, its open
questions or its switching table differ from `goals.md` or `program.md`, the spec wins; the
questions were answered on wayfinder #28 (`docs/spec/research-notes.md`).

**The question.** The v1 scheme registry in `program.md` and its four set types were carried
from the old code without being judged. Which progression methods and set structures does the
evidence support, which should the registry hold, what scheme should adopt fill per emphasis and
exercise kind, and when a line stops responding, which scheme do coaches move it to?

It extends `program-practice.md`, `goal-programming.md` (sections 4 and 5) and
`training-level.md` (sections 2 and 3). Their sources are reused and not repeated here.

**Grades.** Every claim carries one:

- **MA**: a meta-analysis or systematic review.
- **S**: a single study.
- **C**: coach opinion.
- **I**: this note's own inference from the sources, stated as such.

## The short version

- No trial compares progression models head to head per goal. The evidence on schemes is
  indirect: autoregulation is at least as good as percentages for strength, rep progression
  and load progression grow muscle about equally, and periodised beats non-periodised for
  strength by a small margin.
- Every set structure tested against straight sets (drop sets, rest-pause, myo-reps, cluster
  sets) gives about the same hypertrophy. Their case is time, not growth. Rest-pause shows a
  small, inconsistent edge.
- The five v1 schemes earn their place. The evidence argues for adding a training-max
  percentage wave with a rep-out, and for drop-set and myo-rep groups as set structures. The four
  set types cannot express those two structures.
- Coaches change a line's scheme for one reason: the progression cadence has run out after
  resets. A stall on a hypertrophy line is answered with sets, a deload or an exercise swap,
  never a new scheme. The sources say little about which scheme comes after `top_backoff`.

## 1. Catalogue: one row per method

"Reads" is what the method needs from the last Exposure of the line to set this one. "Level" is
the athlete level the sources pair it with. ADR 0014 keeps level out of code, so this column is
for the coach.

| Method | Purpose | Reads | Evidence against straight sets | Level |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Linear load** | Strength and size while the lifter can add load every session | Last load and whether every set was made | No trial against other models. Coaches agree it works while small jumps are possible (C, Helms/Morgan; SS) | Novice, a new exercise, a return from a layoff (C) |
| **Double progression** | Hypertrophy; strength in the 6+ rep range | Reps per set at the target RIR, at a fixed load | Rep progression grew muscle as well as load progression; strength slightly favoured load, 2.0 kg [−2.4, 7.8] (S, Plotkin 2022) | Novice to advanced, at slowing rates (C, Helms/Morgan) |
| **RPE/RIR autoregulation of load** | Strength; keeping effort steady as fitness moves | e1RM or reps at RPE from the last exposure | Similar to fixed load for strength and size (MA, Hickmott 2022). Ranked above percentage loading (network MA, Huang 2025). Small 1RM edge over percentages (S, Helms 2018) | Intermediate and up (C) |
| **Percentage of a training max** | Strength on a fixed schedule; peaking | The training max, moved by the last rep-out or by a test | Ranked last of four loading methods for 1RM (network MA, Huang 2025, abstract only). Works in fixed programs (C, SBS bundle, Wendler) | Intermediate and up (C) |
| **Wave loading** (weekly waves, as in 5/3/1) | Strength at a monthly cadence | The training max | Periodised beats non-periodised for 1RM, ES 0.31–0.43 (MA, Moesgaard 2022, Williams 2017). Undulating beats linear in trained lifters, ES 0.61 (MA, Moesgaard). No hypertrophy effect (MA, Grgic 2017) | Intermediate and advanced (C) |
| **Top set plus back-offs** | Strength stimulus and an e1RM reading in one exposure, then volume | e1RM from the top set; RPE as a cap | No trial. The parts are tested: heavy load for strength (MA, `goal-programming.md` §1), autoregulation (MA, Hickmott) | Intermediate and up (C, BBM) |
| **AMRAP rep-out** | Sets the next load or training max from one set | Reps achieved on the open-top set | APRE, where a rep-out sets the next load, beat linear periodisation (S, Mann 2010); ranked first for 1RM (network MA, Huang 2025) | Any; a test needs safe failure (C) |
| **Rest-pause** | Hypertrophy in less time | Reps in each segment at a fixed load | Hypertrophy about equal, with a small positive coefficient (MA, Tsartsapakis 2026). A larger squat 1RM gain than traditional sets (S, Enes 2021, secondary) | Trained; lower-priority and isolation lifts (C, Baraki, Helms) |
| **Myo-reps** (a standard rest-pause form: one hard set, then mini-sets of 3–5 reps on 15–20 s rest) | Hypertrophy in less time | Reps on the opening set and each mini-set | Same pec growth and bench gain (4.9% vs 4.6%) in about 40% of the session time and 30% less volume load (S, Bradshaw 2026, secondary) | Trained; isolation and lower-priority lifts when time-limited (C, Baraki) |
| **Drop sets** | Hypertrophy in less time | Reps per drop; the load relation between drops | No hypertrophy difference, SMD 0.155, p = 0.392; a half to a third of the time (MA, Sødal 2023). Equal strength; regional rectus femoris gains (S, Varović 2021) | Any; Helms prefers them on single-joint lifts (C, podcast notes) |
| **Cluster sets** | Bar speed and power across a set; less fatigue | Reps per cluster | Similar strength, power, velocity, hypertrophy (ES −0.05 [−0.32, 0.23]) and endurance (MA, Davies 2021) | Power athletes, clinical populations; no case for this athlete (I) |
| **Training to failure** | A stimulus dial for hypertrophy | Whether the set reached failure | No overall difference for strength (ES −0.09) or size (ES 0.22, CI crosses 0). A small size edge in trained lifters, ES 0.15 [0.03, 0.26] (MA, Grgic 2022). 1–2 RIR matched failure for growth with less fatigue (S, Refalo 2024) | Trained; on isolation and machines rather than heavy compounds (C, Helms) |

Three findings cut across the rows:

1. **The set structures save time and do not add muscle.** Tsartsapakis pooled 20 studies.
   Hypertrophy against traditional sets was g 0.046 [−0.057, 0.149]. Strength was g 0.351
   [0.170, 0.533], and no single method stood out (MA). All its drop-set studies left volume
   unequal, so "equal when matched" is weaker than its abstract says.
2. **Rest-pause is the only structure with a positive signal, and it is small.** Tsartsapakis
   found the only significant hypertrophy coefficient for rest-pause (MA). Enes found a squat
   1RM edge (S). Nuckols argues that if "effective reps" mattered, rest-pause and drop sets
   would be much better, and the evidence says they are not (C, SBS).
3. **Where a structure goes depends on the exercise.** Helms keeps compounds at 1–3 RIR and
   saves high-rep and near-failure work for isolation and machines, "when … you won't be
   thrashed" (C). Baraki puts myo-reps on "lower-priority and/or isolation-type movements" and
   advises against using them exclusively (C).

## 2. The registry judged

"Set types" asks whether `warm_up | working | activation | test` can express the method without
a workaround.

| Scheme | Verdict | Reason | Set types express it? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `double_progression` | keep | The hypertrophy default in every coach source; rep progression is supported (S, Plotkin) | Yes |
| `linear` | keep | The agreed model while session-to-session load jumps are possible (C). It is the scheme a `cadence_fast` signal points to | Yes. Its reset (old code 90%) sits inside SS's 5–10% |
| `hold` | keep | Needed for rehab, conditioning lines and the authored `test`. It is not the maintenance evidence, which keeps relative load (MA, Spiering 2021) | Yes |
| `assistance_ladder` | keep | The one way to progress an assisted bodyweight lift with no load to add. The sources say nothing about it | Yes |
| `top_backoff` | keep | The strength and priority-lift default in BBM; autoregulation is supported (MA, Hickmott; network MA, Huang) | Yes, including `test` |
| `rpe_anchored` | drop | The old rule (a top set from e1RM at target reps and RPE, back-offs below it) is `top_backoff` under another name (`parity.md`) | Yes, as `top_backoff` |
| `fixed_reps_rpe` | unsure | By its name and the old `fixed_reps_no_e1rm` warning: fixed sets × reps, load from e1RM at a target RPE. This is the design Helms 2018 tested and the straight-set strength day BBM writes (C). `top_backoff` with back-offs at 100% of the top may already cover it (I) | Yes |
| `wave_531` | add | The training-max percentage wave is the strength cadence after `top_backoff` in the sources that name one (C, Wendler, SBS bundle). `goals.md` already uses it in its worked example. Percentage loading ranks below autoregulation for 1RM (network MA, Huang). Its case is the slower cadence, not better loading | Yes. The last set is a `working` group with an open top; the training max is a Decision |
| `maintenance` | drop | Agreed with `program.md`: the maintenance evidence holds relative load and cuts sets (MA, Spiering), which the emphasis does through dose | Not needed |
| drop sets | add as a set structure, not a scheme | Equal growth in a half to a third of the time (MA, Sødal) | **No.** A drop's load comes from the drop before it, and there is no rest between drops. `rest_s` is per line and a group has one load. Fatigued drops would also give a false e1RM |
| rest-pause and myo-reps | add as a set structure, not a scheme | Equal growth in less time, with a small edge for rest-pause (MA, Tsartsapakis; S, Bradshaw) | **No.** The mini-sets need their own rest of 15–20 s and their own rep target, at the opening set's load. The hard-set count per cluster has no rule. Fagerli's "activation set" is a hard set, while this repo's `activation` counts none |
| cluster sets | do not add | No outcome differs from straight sets for this athlete's goals (MA, Davies) | No; intra-set rest |
| AMRAP rep-out | already present | A `working` group with an open top `"8+"` (`program.md`) | Yes |
| undulating (daily) | not a scheme | One exercise on two sessions is two lines, each with its own rep range, which is already undulation across the week (I) | Yes |

**What Program authoring would have to add**, without designing it:

- A way for a set group to carry its own rest, so that mini-sets can sit beside a full rest.
- A way for a group's load to come from the group before it, as a percent cut.
- A rule for how a drop or rest-pause cluster counts toward hard sets and toward e1RM.
  The likely answer is that only the opening set qualifies for e1RM (I).
- A name for the myo-rep opening set that does not collide with `activation`.

## 3. Defaults per emphasis

The proposed scheme column for `goals.md`. "Kind" is the exercise's equipment kind.
`goals.md` defines the other columns, and this note does not change them.

| Emphasis and line | Barbell compound | Dumbbell | Machine or cable | Bodyweight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `hypertrophy` line | `double_progression`, RIR 3 → 1, no failure (C, Helms) | `double_progression`. Dumbbell jumps are coarse, so reps carry the progress (I from Helms/Morgan: linear needs small jumps) | `double_progression`. An optional drop-set or myo-rep finisher on the last set when time is short (MA, Sødal; C, Baraki, Helms). Not the filled default | `double_progression` on reps; `assistance_ladder` when assisted |
| Priority lift and `strength` line | `top_backoff` (C, BBM; MA, Hickmott) | `top_backoff` only where the rack's steps allow an e1RM-driven top set; otherwise `double_progression` in 3–6 reps (I) | Rare as a priority lift; `top_backoff` (I) | `assistance_ladder` until the lift is unassisted, then `double_progression` on reps, then `top_backoff` once load is added (I; the sources are quiet) |
| `maintenance` version | The Goal's scheme, dose cut | Same | Same | Same |

Notes:

- **The default cannot depend on level.** ADR 0014 stores no level. So `linear` is never the
  filled default, even though every source gives it to novices. A coach chooses it by naming it,
  or moves to it after a `cadence_fast` signal (I).
- **`maintenance` conflicts inside `goals.md`.** Its emphasis text says maintenance "keeps the
  Goal's schemes and intensity and cuts the dose". Its defaults table says "hold load; no step".
  The evidence keeps relative load (MA, Spiering), which an RPE-driven scheme does and `hold`
  does not. This note sides with the emphasis text.
- **Set structures stay off the defaults.** No source makes drop sets, rest-pause or myo-reps a
  default. All present them as a time tool for some exercises (C).

## 4. The switching rule

Rows are stall signals, read per line. "Within the scheme" is what the sources do before
changing anything. "Moves to" is the scheme they change to, if any.

| Stall signal | Scheme | Within the scheme | Moves to | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Missed sets at a load, twice | `linear` | Reset 5% (10% over 40) and climb back | After one reset (BBM) or two to three (SS FAQ) stall again: `double_progression` on a hypertrophy line; a weekly cadence (`top_backoff`) on a strength line | C: SS (Alter), BBM (Feigenbaum), Helms/Morgan. They agree on direction and differ on the number of resets |
| Load stops moving for two or more lifts at once | `linear` | None | The whole program moves to intermediate programming | C, BBM forum |
| Reps not climbing in the range at the target RIR | `double_progression` | Read the trend, not one session. Early in a block, add sets (about 20%). Late in a block, with other lines on the muscle also flat, deload | **None.** At the block boundary, swap the exercise or change the rep range | C: Helms/Morgan, rippedbody plateaus, RP (MRV), BBM. No source moves this line to another scheme |
| e1RM flat or falling over a block | `top_backoff` | Confirm the trend. Check recovery. Change back-off volume. Add a variation for a weak point | After about six weeks flat, a low-stress week and "change the programming stimulus". The sources name no scheme. A weekly wave on a training max (`wave_531`) is the slower cadence the strength sources use | C: BBM article, Baraki (BBM forum), Wendler, SBS. **Sources quiet on the target scheme** |
| Rep-out short of its minimum | `wave_531` | Lower the training max about 10% and stay there "one cycle longer than you think"; "5 forward, 3 back" | Sources quiet | C, Wendler (search-index excerpt) |
| RPE rising at a held load and reps, on many lines | any | A reactive deload: cut sets 30–50%, hold load and RIR | None; this signal is fatigue | C: Helms/Morgan self-check; Delphi consensus (Bell 2023) |
| RPE rising or pain on one line only | any | Check technique. For joint pain, raise reps to 12–20 at the same sets and RPE | An exercise swap, not a scheme change | C, rippedbody plateaus |
| Reps stalled on one rung | `assistance_ladder` | Hold the rung | At zero assistance, `double_progression` on reps | Sources quiet (I) |
| Every exposure at the top of the range, at or under target RIR (`cadence_fast`) | `double_progression`, `top_backoff` | Take the step | `linear` while it holds | C, Helms/Morgan and SS describe the novice pace; the move itself is I |
| Session time over budget (not a stall) | `double_progression` on isolation | None | Add a drop-set or myo-rep structure to that line | MA, Sødal; S, Bradshaw; C, Baraki |

What the sources agree on:

- **A scheme changes when its cadence runs out, not when one week goes badly.** The shape is
  N misses at a load, R resets, then a slower cadence (C; `training-level.md` §2).
- **The order is the same everywhere.** Confirm the trend, check recovery, adjust volume or the
  training max, add variation, and swap the exercise last (C, every source).
- **A hypertrophy line never changes scheme on a stall.** Its stall is answered by dose, deload
  or exercise (C).

Where they disagree or go quiet:

- **The number of resets before the switch.** Starting Strength allows one to three, BBM one.
- **The scheme after `top_backoff`.** No source names it. Moving to a percentage wave is a
  cadence choice that the loading evidence does not favour (network MA, Huang).
- **Bodyweight progression past the ladder.** No source was found.
- **Whether a set structure should rescue a stalled line.** Nuckols says the evidence does not
  support it (C). No source recommends drop sets or rest-pause as a stall response.

## Open questions for the grilling ticket

1. Should `fixed_reps_rpe` return as its own scheme, or is it `top_backoff` with back-offs at
   100% of the top?
2. Should `wave_531` enter v1 as the named target for a `cadence_slow` strength lift? Or should
   that target stay the coach's call with no default?
3. Drop sets and myo-reps need per-group rest, a load relation between groups and a hard-set
   rule. Are they worth that cost in v1, or are they deferred and authored as plain working sets?
4. Which name replaces Fagerli's "activation set", so that it does not collide with
   `activation`?
5. How many resets under `linear` before the switch: one (BBM) or two (SS)? Is it the same
   for every lift?
6. Should the `maintenance` defaults row say "the Goal's scheme, dose cut" instead of
   "hold load; no step"?
7. Should the `program` skill propose `linear` on a `cadence_fast` signal, given that level is
   not stored?
8. When a dumbbell priority lift's rack steps are too coarse for `top_backoff`, should adopt
   fall back to `double_progression` and warn?

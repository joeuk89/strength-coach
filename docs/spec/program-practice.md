# How coaches write and evolve a program

Research note for wayfinder #14. It describes what practising strength coaches hold fixed, what they move weekly, and why, in the glossary's provisional terms (Program, Block, WeekPlan, Prescription). Each claim marks whether it is evidence or practitioner opinion.

## The shape coaches use

Every source organises training as nested cycles: a microcycle (usually one week), a mesocycle of two to six microcycles, and longer blocks or macrocycles above that ([NSCA](https://nsca.com/education/articles/kinetic-select/hierarchical-structure-of-periodization-cycles), [JTS](https://www.jtsstrength.com/the-terms-of-the-deal/)). JTS defines a mesocycle as "an organized sequence of microcycles ordered to elicit a set of distinct training adaptations", typically three to five accumulation weeks plus one deload week, with 3:1 the classic ratio and 4:1 common in hypertrophy work (opinion, [JTS](https://www.jtsstrength.com/the-terms-of-the-deal/)). RP's hypertrophy mesocycle is the same shape: four to six accumulation weeks then one deload week (opinion, [RP](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/progressing-for-hypertrophy)).

The evidence behind the shape is modest. Periodised training beats non-periodised for strength by a small, consistent margin, larger in trained lifters, and undulating beats linear only in trained lifters ([Nuckols' review of about 60 studies](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/periodization/)). For hypertrophy, linear and daily-undulating models are indistinguishable: 13 studies, pooled d = −0.02, CI [−0.25, 0.21] ([Grgic 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5571788/)). The specific week counts and ratios are coaching convention, not research cut-offs.

## Fixed for a block

Coaches hold three things constant for the length of a mesocycle.

- **Exercise selection.** RP rotates exercises only between mesocycles. Barbell Medicine names swapping too often as a common mistake because "each new exercise resets the conditions under which performance is measured" ([BBM](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/blog/progress-chest-training-hypertrophy/)). Helms cites studies where lifters who swapped exercises weekly gained less muscle ([Helms via Boostcamp](http://www.boostcamp.app/blogs/modify-your-training-program)). The systematic review agrees: planned variation across muscle regions helps, "excessive rotation of different exercises (i.e., high frequency of change)" harms, and variation on skill-based strength lifts is unfavourable ([Kassiano 2022](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-7/exercise-variation-for-strength-and-hypertrophy-change-is-not-always-good/), evidence).
- **Rep range and proximity-to-failure scheme.** Each phase has its range: JTS gives 5–10 for hypertrophy, 3–6 for strength, 1–3 for peaking (opinion, [JTS](https://www.jtsstrength.com/nonsense-periodization-powerlifting/)). The SBS bundle fixes a block at seven weeks and shifts the range and intensity only at block boundaries ([SBS](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/program-bundle/)).
- **The progression model.** The rule that turns last week's result into this week's target is chosen once per block: linear load jumps, double progression within a rep range, RIR-driven autoregulation, or percentage of a training max ([Helms/Morgan](https://rippedbody.com/progression/), [SBS](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/program-bundle/)).

## What changes weekly

Within a block only the dose moves, and it moves by rule.

- **Load.** Barbell Medicine's default is to raise estimated 1RM each week when possible, planning 1–5 lb or 1–5% above last week and letting the day's RPE override the plan: "let RPE supersede our plans" ([BBM forum, coach answers](https://forum.barbellmedicine.com/t/clarification-on-weekly-progression-using-templates/5667)). e1RM is "calculated based on your actual performance during the session", never set by hand ([BBM forum](https://forum.barbellmedicine.com/t/how-much-should-e1rm-increase-from-week-to-week/10913)). JTS says starting loads must leave room to add weight for the whole block ([JTS](https://www.jtsstrength.com/selecting-and-progressing-your-weights/)).
- **Reps.** Under double progression, reps climb within the range first, then load goes up and reps fall back to the bottom. Progress is judged on trends, and a weak week is normal ([Helms/Morgan](https://rippedbody.com/progression/)).
- **Sets and RIR.** RP starts a mesocycle near minimum effective volume, adds sets each week until performance drops, and walks RIR down from about 4 to 1 across the accumulation weeks (opinion, [RP](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/in-defense-of-set-increases-within-the-hypertrophy-mesocycle)).
- **Nothing else.** Progress can show as more reps, steadier execution or better recovery at the same load, so a flat load is not a stall ([BBM](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/blog/progress-chest-training-hypertrophy/)).

## When an exercise is swapped or added

Practitioners agree on three triggers and reject a fourth.

1. **Block boundary.** The planned place for new exercises, including introducing next block's lifts during the deload (95% coach agreement, [Bell 2023 Delphi](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10511399/)).
2. **Pain or missing kit.** Swap like for like within the movement pattern, keeping the rep range and RPE target: a barbell bench with shoulder pain becomes a dumbbell bench ([Helms](http://www.boostcamp.app/blogs/modify-your-training-program)). On a strength program the swap must preserve transfer to the target lift, so it is a coach's call, not the athlete's.
3. **A stall over a meaningful timeframe.** Not one session. Options are more volume, redistributed work, or a new exercise, in that order ([BBM](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/blog/progress-chest-training-hypertrophy/)).

Rejected: swapping because a week went badly, or for novelty ("muscle confusion").

## Deload and re-test

The coach consensus definition of a deload is "a period of reduced training stress designed to mitigate physiological and psychological fatigue" ([Bell 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10511399/)). Thirty-four coaches agreed (100%) that a deload can be pre-planned, autoregulated, or both, sits between mesocycles or whenever the athlete is fatigued, and reduces days, sets or reps while keeping exercises familiar and the main lifts in. They reached no consensus on a fixed length or interval; "every 4–6 weeks" comes from the background literature, not the panel. Helms/Morgan make it reactive, cutting sets 30–50% while keeping load and effort ([rippedbody](https://rippedbody.com/progression/)); JTS cuts reps by half and keeps the weight heavy ([JTS](https://www.jtsstrength.com/nonsense-periodization-powerlifting/)). The sources differ on the cut, not on when.

A formal re-test is a strength-sport device: a realisation week with an AMRAP or heavy single that sets the next block's training max. Hypertrophy coaches do not re-test; the next block's starting loads come from "your previous block" and are adjusted "by feel on the day" ([JTS](https://www.jtsstrength.com/selecting-and-progressing-your-weights/)), or from the logged e1RM (BBM). For this athlete, intermediate and recreational, the re-test is the logged performance, not an event.

## Implications for the domain model

**Fits practice.**

- **Program** as the block-level fixture: sessions, exercise selection, rep range and RIR scheme, progression model, block length and deload policy. Versioned and immutable once adopted matches "fixed for the mesocycle". A new version is cut at a block boundary or for a pain or kit swap.
- **Block** as the mesocycle: a counted run of hard weeks under one program version, ended by a deload or reset. The Delphi consensus supports both a ceiling and fatigue triggers, which is what the old code did.
- **Prescription** as the week's concrete numbers per exercise: load, reps, sets, RPE or RIR target, derived from the program's rule and the latest logged exposure.

**Tensions.**

- **Is a weekly load bump a WeekPlan or progression state?** In practice it is neither a plan edit nor a program edit: it is the progression rule applied to new facts. Every source derives this week's load from last week's log (e1RM, reps at RIR, sets completed). That argues for Prescription being a *derived* value, `prescribe(program_version, history, date)`, with no stored weekly delta at all. Storing a weekly load row would copy derived data, against the "derived data never stored" principle, and reintroduces the #257 double-cut: a stored delta over a stored delta.
- **What WeekPlan is for, then.** Practice has exactly three kinds of weekly departure: a deload or light week, a disrupted week (travel, illness, missed sessions, return band), and a one-off session swap for pain or kit. All three are overrides *to the rule*, not to the numbers: "sets ×0.5, load held", "return band −10%", "exercise X → Y for this week". WeekPlan fits if it is a small set of typed overrides over the program for a date span, applied before derivation, and the approval binds to the derived prescriptions it produces. It does not fit if it carries every week's loads.
- **Exercise swap: WeekPlan or Program version?** Practice splits it. A pain swap for one week is a WeekPlan override. A swap that persists is a new program version, because it resets the performance baseline for that slot (BBM). The model needs both, and a rule for promoting one to the other.
- **ProgressionState is per lift, not per program.** e1RM, best reps-at-RIR and the double-progression position belong to an exercise-in-slot and are recomputed from history. If stored at all it is a cache, never an input.
- **Block end is an event, not a week.** The Delphi panel's "deload whenever fatigued" means the block boundary is decided at check-in, not pre-dated. The old code's "count resets when the check-in closes the week" is right.

**Does not fit.** A WeekPlan that is "a delta over a program version" with no restriction on what the delta may touch. Coaches never edit the plan weekly; they apply a rule, or override the rule for a named reason.

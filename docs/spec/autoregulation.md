## Answer

RPE targets tied to reps in reserve work for setting and adjusting loads:
lifters who choose loads to an RPE target gain at least as much strength
as those lifting fixed percentages. Ratings are most accurate near failure
and in shorter sets, which is where most working sets in this framework
sit. A compound lift rated 1 or more over target on two or more sets, in
two sessions running, is read as fatigue rather than a bad day.

## Evidence

**Claim 1.** hickmott-2022 pooled RPE-based and velocity-based load
autoregulation and found "no significant differences between autoregulated
and standardized load prescription" for 1RM strength (mean difference
2.07 kg, p = 0.09); only one included study measured hypertrophy. In
helms-2018, trained men who picked loads to RPE targets for 8 weeks gained
as much squat and bench strength as a percentage group, with small effect
sizes favouring RPE, and the same gain in muscle thickness. larsen-2021
reviewed 14 studies and found every autoregulation protocol, RIR-based RPE
included, raised 1RM. zourdos-2016 introduced the RIR-based scale (RPE 10
is 0 reps in reserve, RPE 9 is 1, and so on) and found ratings tracked bar
speed closely (r = −0.88 in experienced squatters).

**Claim 2.** halperin-2022 pooled 12 studies (414 participants).
Participants underpredicted reps to failure by about 1 rep (0.95) on
average, with wide variation. Accuracy improved when predictions were made
closer to failure and when sets to failure were 12 reps or fewer.
helms-2016 makes the same point from earlier trials: the closer to
failure, the more accurate the estimate.

**Claims 3 and 7.** Claim 3 said ratings grow more accurate with
experience. zourdos-2016 found experienced squatters rated a true 1RM
higher (closer to RPE 10) than novices did, but halperin-2022, the largest
and most recent analysis, found training status "did not seem to influence
prediction accuracy" (β = −0.006 reps), while predictions made closer to
failure were more accurate. Claim 7 replaces claim 3 and keeps what the
evidence supports: accuracy improves near failure, and helms-2016 advises
lifters new to the scale to practise rating before relying on it. Claim 7 is a coaching heuristic at low confidence: no trial
tests whether practising near failure makes later ratings more accurate.

**Claim 4.** No study tests this drift rule. larsen-2021 frames
autoregulation as adjusting to daily fitness, fatigue and readiness, which
is the logic behind reading repeated over-target RPE as fatigue. The
threshold of 1 RPE sits just above the average rating error in
halperin-2022, and requiring two sets and two sessions filters out a
single misjudged set. The scope (compound lifts and sets under a recovery
cap) is this framework's choice, and so is the eligibility: a set made
harder than prescribed, an extra set or deliberate recovery work says
nothing about fatigue at the prescription, and two over-target exposures
with no earlier one at the prescription are as likely a load set too
heavy as fatigue. No study validates these gates; they are application
policy.

**Claim 5.** reynolds-2006 tested 70 adults at 1, 5, 10 and 20RM on the
bench press and leg press. Load fell off non-linearly as reps rose, 5RM
sets predicted 1RM best, and the authors advise using no more than 10
reps in linear equations; Epley is one. The framework counts reps in
reserve as reps, so a 10-rep set at RPE 8 is 12 reps to failure. A cap
of 10 would give those lines no e1RM, and `fixed_reps_rpe` sets loads
only from an e1RM. The cap of 12 is the edge of claim 2's accuracy range;
no study tests it, and points near 12 carry more error than points from
heavy sets.

**Claim 6.** No study tests either threshold. 10.5 leaves half an RPE
beyond failure for rounding and estimate error before a load counts as
unearned. The tolerance of 1 RPE matches claim 4's margin, just above the
average rating error in halperin-2022. lesuer-1997 tested seven 1RM
equations on 67 untrained college students: predictions correlated
highly with measured 1RM, but every equation, Epley included,
significantly underestimated the deadlift 1RM. An e1RM that is too low
makes a sound load look heavier, so its implied RPE runs high when the
e1RM came from longer sets than the prescription. `unearned_load` or
`rpe_infeasible` can then fire on a hinge lift that is in fact on track.

## Applying it

- Prescribe working sets with an RPE target and let the athlete adjust
  the load to hit it; this is the framework's default form of
  autoregulation.
- Trust ratings most on heavy, short sets near failure. Treat RPE on long
  sets and on sets far from failure as rough, and don't progress or hold
  a lift on that alone.
- `/check-in` and `/program-week` apply claim 4 to compound lifts and
  recovery-capped sets. Isolation drift is noted, not acted on. Drift
  with no earlier exposure at the prescription is shown as calibration,
  so the coach reviews the load instead of proposing a light week.
- With a lifter new to RPE, have them practise rating sets near failure
  before leaning on RPE targets (claim 7). Expect ratings to run low (more
  reps left than they think) at any experience level.
- Keep claim 5's low-confidence cap at 12 reps to failure. Preserve
  over-cap observations with an explicit unavailable-estimate reason.
  Compare e1RMs from ordinary exposures only; light-week, return-week and
  reduced work stays visible without becoming a strength loss. A newer
  over-cap exposure of ordinary or return-week work, at or above the load
  behind an older estimate, makes that estimate unavailable as a
  progression veto, while the other
  prescription guards remain active. These are model-use policies, not
  physiological thresholds established by Reynolds or Halperin.
- Claim 6 judges a rep range by its best-fitting whole rep target and
  a recovery RPE (deload, light, return or capped work) as a ceiling. A
  set logged without RPE gives an e1RM that counts every rep as taken to
  failure, so the estimate is a lower bound: it cannot show a load beyond
  failure, and the load-step guard judges the step instead. The 10.5 and
  ±1 thresholds are unchanged heuristics.
- Before lowering a deadlift or other hinge load for `unearned_load` or
  `rpe_infeasible`, check the athlete's recent logged sets of that lift:
  the flag may come from Epley's low estimate rather than the load.

## Open questions

- Does practice with the RIR scale itself improve accuracy, as distinct
  from years of lifting? halperin-2022 tested training status, not
  practice with the scale.
- Is RPE drift across sessions a better fatigue signal than a drop in
  reps at a fixed load? No trial compares them.
- Does Epley underestimate the deadlift 1RM in trained lifters who log
  RPE, as it did in lesuer-1997's untrained students? If `unearned_load`
  misfires on deadlifts in real check-ins, make the feasibility flags
  lift-specific.

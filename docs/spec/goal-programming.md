# How the athlete's goal changes what a coach programs

Research note for wayfinder #25: what the athlete's goal (hypertrophy, strength, concurrent strength + hypertrophy, maintenance) changes in a program, with the evidence behind each change, in the glossary's terms (Program version, Emphasis, Line, Prescription, WeekPlan, Block, Decision). Where the Implications below differ from `goals.md`, the spec wins (`docs/spec/research-notes.md`). It extends `program-practice.md`, which already covers mesocycle shape, what moves weekly, swaps and deloads in general; nothing there is repeated. Claims are marked evidence or opinion.

The short version: the evidence separates the goals on four knobs and almost nothing else. Load (% 1RM) and proximity to failure split strength from hypertrophy cleanly. Volume has a different dose-response for each. Rest and exercise order follow from load. Progression model, stall handling and deload shape are coaching convention, chosen per goal for reasons of fatigue and measurement, not because a trial showed one wins.

## 1. Rep range and intensity

**Strength is load-specific; hypertrophy is not.** This is the best-supported claim in the note.

- Schoenfeld's 2017 meta-analysis of 21 studies: 1RM gains favour high load (>60% 1RM) over low load; isometric strength and hypertrophy do not differ (evidence, [Schoenfeld 2017](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28834797)).
- Lopez's network meta-analysis of 28 studies, 747 adults: hypertrophy does not differ by load; high and moderate load beat low load for strength (SMD 0.60–0.63 and 0.34–0.35); high over moderate is a non-significant SMD 0.26–0.28, p = 0.068 (evidence, [Lopez 2021](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/33433148)).
- Carvalho's volume-load-matched meta-analysis: for 1RM, high (≥80%) beats moderate (60–79%) beats low (30–59%); for hypertrophy no difference between any category (evidence, [Carvalho 2022](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/35015560)).
- Currier's Bayesian network meta-analysis, 178 strength studies and 119 hypertrophy studies: higher-load (>80% 1RM) prescriptions maximise strength, "all prescriptions comparably promoted muscle hypertrophy"; the top hypertrophy prescription was higher-load, multi-set, twice weekly, so heavy work is not a hypertrophy cost (evidence, [Currier 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10579494/)).
- The direct test in trained men: 2–4 reps vs 8–12 reps, 3 sets × 7 exercises × 3 days × 8 weeks. Squat 1RM +30.0% heavy vs +16.8% moderate (p = 0.03); lateral thigh thickness +4.1% heavy vs +10.4% moderate (p = 0.007). With *sets* equated rather than volume load, moderate loads grew more muscle; with volume load equated (Carvalho) they do not (evidence, [Schoenfeld 2016 JSSM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5131226/)).

**The "hypertrophy range" is a practical range, not a physiological one.** Schoenfeld's re-examination of the repetition continuum puts the hypertrophy floor "somewhere in the range of 30% 1RM", says there is "no ideal hypertrophy zone in principle", and recommends moderate loads as the time-efficient default because light-load sets need many more reps and heavy-load sets need more sets for the same growth (evidence with opinion on efficiency, [Schoenfeld 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7927075/)). Nuckols estimates the moderate-range edge at "maybe a ~10-15% advantage per unit of effort invested at most", and suggests 60–70% of a muscle's work sets in the lifter's best range with 15–20% each heavier and lighter (opinion, [SBS](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/hypertrophy-range-fact-fiction/)).

**Coach defaults.** JTS: 5–10 reps hypertrophy, 3–6 strength, 1–3 peaking (opinion, [JTS](https://www.jtsstrength.com/nonsense-periodization-powerlifting/)). NSCA's textbook table: strength ≥85% 1RM for ≤6 reps, hypertrophy 67–85% for 6–12 (opinion, secondary summary of Essentials Table 17.9 via [NSCA Coach](https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/nsca-coach/using-intensity-based-on-sets-and-repetitions-over-50-years-of-experience-a-brief-overview-of-load-setting-and-programming-strategy/)). Helms/Morgan give no fixed range and prescribe by RIR, treating the %1RM-to-reps table as "a framework only" (opinion, [rippedbody](https://rippedbody.com/rpe/)). Confidence: high that strength needs ≥80% work; high that hypertrophy is load-indifferent above ~30%; the exact rep bands are convention.

**Maintenance.** The maintenance evidence (section 3) holds *relative load* and cuts volume, so maintenance keeps the goal's rep range and intensity, not a lighter one (evidence, [Spiering 2021](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/33629972)).

## 2. Proximity to failure

**Hypertrophy responds to RIR; strength does not.** Robinson's meta-regressions (RIR as a continuous estimate): for strength the RIR slopes' intervals include zero; for hypertrophy they are negative and exclude zero, so growth rises as sets approach failure (evidence, [Robinson 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2)). Refalo's 2023 meta-analysis of 15 studies found a small edge for set failure over non-failure (ES 0.19, CI 0.00–0.37, p = 0.045) and no advantage for momentary failure once failure is defined strictly (ES 0.12, CI −0.13 to 0.37), suggesting a non-linear curve that flattens near failure (evidence, [Refalo 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-022-01784-y)). Refalo's 2024 trial in trained lifters: 1–2 RIR produced the same quadriceps growth as momentary failure (0.182 vs 0.181 cm) with less neuromuscular fatigue (evidence, [Refalo 2024](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/38393985)).

**What that gives per goal.**

- Hypertrophy: 0–3 RIR on working sets, drifting toward 1 across a block. RP walks RIR from ~4 to ~1 over a mesocycle (opinion, cited in program-practice.md); Nuckols: "within 2-3 reps of failure" (opinion, [SBS](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/hypertrophy-range-fact-fiction/)); Helms/Morgan: "close enough to failure for the set to provide a meaningful growth stimulus, especially at lower loads" (opinion, [rippedbody](https://rippedbody.com/rpe/)).
- Strength: RIR is a fatigue dial, not a stimulus dial. "Your 1RM isn't influenced by how close you are to failure in each set" (opinion resting on Robinson, [rippedbody](https://rippedbody.com/rpe/)). BBM's strength templates top out at RPE 8–9 on the top set with back-offs "in the RPE 5-6 range" (opinion, [BBM forum, Feigenbaum](https://forum.barbellmedicine.com/t/how-to-incorporate-top-sets/8800)). JTS keeps strength work "just shy of failure" so reps can be matched week to week (opinion, [JTS](https://www.jtsstrength.com/nonsense-periodization-powerlifting/)).
- Maintenance: keep the goal's RIR; the dose cut is in sets (section 3).

Confidence: high on the direction (hypertrophy needs closeness, strength does not); moderate on the exact RIR numbers, which are coach convention sitting on a flattening curve.

## 3. Weekly set volume per muscle

**Hypertrophy: graded dose-response with a wide, flat top.**

- Schoenfeld 2017 meta-regression, 34 groups from 15 studies: each added weekly set is worth ES +0.023, about 0.37% more growth; ≥10 sets/week gained about 3.9% more than <5 (evidence, [Schoenfeld 2017](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27433992)).
- Baz-Valle's review in trained men: moderate (12–20 sets/week) and high (>20) did not differ for quadriceps or biceps; high beat moderate for triceps; the authors recommend 12–20 sets per muscle per week for trained men (evidence, [Baz-Valle 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8884877/)).
- Pelland's meta-regression, 67 studies, 2,058 participants, with indirect sets counted at 0.5: posterior probability 100% of a positive volume slope for both hypertrophy and strength, with diminishing returns "considerably more pronounced" for strength; frequency has a consistent effect only on strength. Preprint, not peer reviewed (evidence, [Pelland 2024](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460)).
- RP's landmarks (MV, MEV, MAV, MRV) are the coaching layer on this curve: start a mesocycle near MEV, add sets weekly, and treat a performance drop as the MRV signal that ends the block (opinion, [RP](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/training-volume-landmarks-muscle-growth); the page was rate-limited this session and the definitions are taken from RP's set-increases article cited in program-practice.md).

**Strength: a shallower curve that saturates early.** Ralston's meta-analysis of nine studies: high weekly sets (≥10 per exercise) over low (≤5) is ES 0.18 (CI 0.06–0.30) combined, and 0.14 (CI −0.01 to 0.29, p = 0.06) for exercise-specific 1RM; medium (5–9) over low is 0.15. The authors call the gain "marginal" and say for well-trained lifters "either MWS or HWS may be an appropriate dose" (evidence, [Ralston 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5684266/)). Pelland's stronger diminishing returns for strength agrees. Androulakis-Korakakis: one set per lift, 1–3 times a week at 70–85% 1RM raised 1RM in trained men (pooled +12.1 kg; squat +17.5, bench +8.3) in six studies, with "suboptimal" magnitude against higher doses (evidence, [Androulakis-Korakakis 2020](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/31797219)). So the strength minimum effective dose is about 2–6 hard sets per lift per week, and the useful range is roughly 5–15 sets per lift, counted per lift rather than per muscle.

**Maintenance: a third or less, with intensity kept.** Bickel's 32-week trial: after 16 weeks of 3×/week training, young adults kept muscle size on one-ninth of the dose (one session per week, one set per exercise) and kept strength on either one-third or one-ninth; older adults needed one-third to keep fibre size but kept strength on both (evidence, [Bickel 2011](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/21131862)). Spiering's review: strength and size in younger adults held for up to 32 weeks on one session per week, one set per exercise, "if relative load was kept"; older adults may need two sessions and 2–3 sets; "intensity appears to be the most important variable for maintenance" (evidence, [Spiering 2021](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/33629972)). Delphi panel: a minimum effective volume dose can be used for a deload (90.5%) and deloading can maintain fitness (89.5%) (evidence of consensus, [Bell 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10511399/)). RP's MV is the same idea as a coaching landmark (opinion).

Confidence: high on direction for all three; the hypertrophy landmark numbers (10–20) are moderate; the strength numbers are low (nine studies, mostly novices); the maintenance numbers are moderate and from untrained-to-trained populations, so the floor for a trained lifter may be higher.

## 4. Progression model

No trial compares progression models head to head per goal. What exists:

- **RPE/RIR autoregulation vs percentages for strength.** Helms 2018, trained men, sets and reps matched, 8 weeks: RPE loading gave a small 1RM edge (79%, 57% and 72% chance of a small effect for squat, bench and total) (evidence, [Helms 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5877330/)). Hickmott's meta-analysis of 15 studies: autoregulated and standardised load gave similar strength; subjective (RIR) and objective (velocity) autoregulation were similar; for hypertrophy, velocity-loss thresholds above 20–25% grew more muscle because they accumulate more volume (evidence, [Hickmott 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8762534/)). So autoregulation is at least as good as percentages for strength and is the only model that encodes "closeness to failure" for hypertrophy.
- **Periodisation for strength only.** Volume-equated: periodised beats non-periodised for 1RM (ES 0.31, CI 0.04–0.57) and undulating beats linear in trained lifters (ES 0.61); hypertrophy shows no effect of either (ES 0.13 and 0.05, both crossing zero) (evidence, [Moesgaard 2022](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/35044672)). Varied loading zones across the week (2–4, 8–12, 20–30) vs constant 8–12 in trained men: no significant differences, effect sizes slightly favouring varied (evidence, [Schoenfeld 2016 IJSM](https://www.thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/abstract/10.1055/s-0035-1569369)).

**Which model each goal uses, and why (opinion, coach sources).**

| Model | Fits | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Linear load (add weight each session) | Novice, any goal | Helms/Morgan: works "where you can make incremental weight increases each session"; lasts six months to years ([rippedbody](https://rippedbody.com/progression/)). JTS raises hypertrophy loads 5–10 lb a week ([JTS](https://www.jtsstrength.com/nonsense-periodization-powerlifting/)). |
| Double progression (reps up within a range at a fixed RIR, then load) | Hypertrophy, intermediate | Progress is reps or load "to keep the same proximity to failure"; intermediates add reps in the 10–20 range week to week and load month to month ([rippedbody](https://rippedbody.com/progression/)). BBM's powerbuilding uses "RPE and double progression" for the hypertrophy lines ([BBM](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/shop/training-templates/strength/powerbuilding-i-template/)). |
| RIR/RPE autoregulation of load | Strength, intermediate and up | Strength progresses "by increasing the load as you get stronger to maintain an appropriate intensity" ([rippedbody](https://rippedbody.com/progression/)); BBM derives next week's load from this week's e1RM (program-practice.md); Helms 2018 above. |
| Percentage of a training max | Strength, fixed-schedule programs | SBS's 21-week programs move % 1RM up and reps down by block, and move the training max by the last set's result ([SBS](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/program-bundle/)). A training max is a Decision in this engine. |
| Top set + back-offs | Strength, and powerbuilding on priority lifts | One heavy set at RPE 8–9 for the strength stimulus and the e1RM reading, then lighter sets for volume at RPE 5–6 (BBM strength 3: a single at RPE 8, 5 at RPE 9, then 20 at 76% e1RM) ([BBM forum](https://forum.barbellmedicine.com/t/question-of-intensity-between-strength-and-hypertrophy-templates/10879), [BBM forum](https://forum.barbellmedicine.com/t/how-to-incorporate-top-sets/8800)). Feigenbaum is sceptical of it as a *hypertrophy* scheme: four sets of 8–12 at RPE 8 is his hypertrophy squat day. |
| Straight sets | Hypertrophy, and strength volume work | Equal sets at one load and RIR; the double-progression unit. |

Confidence: moderate that autoregulation suits strength; the rest is convention with a clear rationale (what each goal measures: e1RM for strength, reps-at-RIR and set count for hypertrophy).

## 5. Stall handling

No study defines a stall or tests responses. Coach practice splits by what the goal measures.

**Strength lift stalled** (e1RM flat or falling over several exposures): diagnose before changing anything. BBM: "Short-term performance fluctuations are normal and do not necessarily indicate a true plateau"; review "training volume, intensity, exercise selection, fatigue, recovery, and performance trends over time", and act on the cause: raise the stimulus if volume or intensity is too low, let fatigue dissipate "while maintaining sufficient stimulus" if it is high, add a variation if the training does not target the tested lift (opinion, [BBM](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/blog/strength-training-plateau/)). SBS's programs reduce the training max when the last set misses its target and raise it when it is beaten, so a stall is absorbed by the rule week to week; the program page gives the mechanism but not the thresholds (opinion, [SBS](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/program-bundle/)). Helms/Morgan: first "determining whether you're actually plateaued", "in terms of trends, not single sessions" (opinion, [rippedbody](https://rippedbody.com/progression/)). The order of moves, in every source: confirm the trend, check recovery, then adjust volume or the training max, then variation, and last swap the exercise. The coach decides the trigger; the timeframe is weeks, not sessions (program-practice.md).

**Hypertrophy line stalled** (reps at the target RIR flat or falling): the signal means something different. Under RP's model a performance drop across the muscle's lines is the MRV signal and ends the block with a deload, so the response is fewer sets, not more (opinion, [RP](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/training-volume-landmarks-muscle-growth)). BBM's hypertrophy advice: a flat load is not a stall when reps, execution or recovery improve; before swapping, try more volume or redistributed work (opinion, cited in program-practice.md). The two are consistent once the week in the block is known: early in a block a flat line wants more sets; late in a block, with other lines also flat, it wants the deload.

**Maintenance**: a stall is not an event. The check is whether strength on the kept lifts holds; a fall over a block is the trigger to raise the dose toward the goal's minimum (opinion, following Spiering).

Confidence: low on any specific rule; high that the response differs by goal because the measured quantity differs.

## 6. Deload shape

program-practice.md covers when and the Delphi consensus. What the goal adds:

- **Which variable to cut.** The panel agreed cutting days (100%), sets (100%) or reps (100%) all reduce volume, that intensity "can stay the same while volume drops" (95.2%) and "may remain high" (81.0%), and did not agree any percentage (evidence of consensus, [Bell 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10511399/)). Hypertrophy coaches cut sets 30–50% and keep load and RIR (opinion, [rippedbody](https://rippedbody.com/progression/)); JTS's hypertrophy deload halves reps and keeps weight heavy every 4–6 weeks, and the strength phase takes "1-2 deloads in there as needed" (opinion, [JTS](https://www.jtsstrength.com/nonsense-periodization-powerlifting/)). The common thread for both goals is: keep the load, cut the dose. Nobody cuts load for a planned deload; a load cut is a return band or an illness cap.
- **Does the deload cost anything?** The only trial: a one-week complete break mid-way through nine weeks cost lower-body strength against continuous training and did not change hypertrophy, power or endurance (evidence, [Coleman 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10809978/)). That is a full rest week in novices, not a volume deload, but it is the reason strength programs deload lighter and later than hypertrophy programs (opinion).
- **Formal re-test / realisation week.** Strength-sport only, as program-practice.md says. BBM's powerbuilding ends two 5-week blocks with "a testing week" (opinion, [BBM](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/shop/training-templates/strength/powerbuilding-i-template/)); JTS's peaking phase is 1–3 reps building to meet attempts (opinion, [JTS](https://www.jtsstrength.com/nonsense-periodization-powerlifting/)). The Delphi panel made no statement about testing. Hypertrophy and maintenance have no re-test; the logged e1RM is the reading (ADR 0005).

Confidence: high that intensity is held; low on the amount; high that re-tests belong to strength.

## 7. Exercise priority and order

- **Order matters for strength on the lift done first, not for hypertrophy.** Nunes's meta-analysis of 11 studies: overall order has no effect on strength (ES −0.11), but multi-joint first favours multi-joint strength (ES 0.32, p = 0.034) and single-joint first favours single-joint strength (ES −0.58, p = 0.032); hypertrophy is unaffected (ES 0.03, p = 0.862) (evidence, [Nunes 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/17461391.2020.1733672)). So under a strength goal the priority lift goes first because the gain is specific to it; under hypertrophy the order is free and is chosen for fatigue and preference.
- **NSCA's convention**: power exercises, then other multi-joint ("core") lifts, then assistance (opinion, NSCA Essentials via secondary summary; no primary page found this session).
- **Interference within a session.** No trial tests heavy strength work followed by hypertrophy accessories against the same work split across days. The indirect evidence is Nunes (whatever is first gains most) and the rest-interval work (heavy sets need longer rest, section 9). Coach practice puts the heavy top set first, back-offs second, accessories last, and limits accessory volume on the heavy day (opinion, BBM templates above; JTS strength phase chooses assistance "to match the competition lifts' movement patterns"). Schoenfeld's 2014 volume-load-equated comparison is the caution: 7×3 built the same muscle as 3×10 and more strength, but the sessions took much longer (evidence, [Schoenfeld 2014](https://bretcontreras.com/wp-content/uploads/Effects-of-different-volume-equated-resistance-training-loading-strategies-on-muscular-adaptations-in-well-trained-men.pdf)).

Confidence: moderate on order-specificity; low on in-session interference.

## 8. Concurrent strength + hypertrophy

**They train together.** Size predicts strength more as training age rises: lean-mass gains explained about 35% of squat gains, 46% of bench and 65% of the combined total in trained lifters, and muscle thickness explains 65–90% of strength between elite powerlifters (evidence summarised, [SBS size vs strength](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/size-vs-strength/)). Heavy work builds muscle (Currier's top hypertrophy prescription is high-load) and moderate work builds strength (Lopez: moderate beats low; high over moderate non-significant), so neither goal's scheme is wasted on the other. The one thing heavy work adds that moderate cannot is 1RM specificity; the one thing moderate adds is more growth per set at lower joint cost (Schoenfeld 2016 JSSM, Schoenfeld 2021). Mixing them within a week costs nothing measurable: varied vs constant loading in trained men showed no differences, with effect sizes slightly favouring varied (evidence, [Schoenfeld 2016 IJSM](https://www.thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/abstract/10.1055/s-0035-1569369)).

**The three structures coaches use (all opinion).**

1. *Heavy top set then back-off sets on the same lift.* BBM's strength and powerbuilding templates: a top single or set at RPE 8–9, then back-offs at RPE 5–6 or ~74–76% of e1RM for 5–8 reps ([BBM forum](https://forum.barbellmedicine.com/t/question-of-intensity-between-strength-and-hypertrophy-templates/10879)). The top set is the strength stimulus and the e1RM reading; the back-offs are the volume.
2. *Strength scheme on 1–3 priority lifts, hypertrophy on everything else.* BBM powerbuilding: squat, bench and deadlift trained "alongside hypertrophy work" with "targeted accessory work" progressed by double progression; two 5-week blocks "developmental → more specific strength work", then a testing week ([BBM](https://www.barbellmedicine.com/shop/training-templates/strength/powerbuilding-i-template/)). SBS's bundle offers strength and hypertrophy *versions* of the same 21-week structure, differing in how fast reps fall per block, and says "people have gotten great strength and hypertrophy results on all 6 programs" ([SBS](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/program-bundle/)).
3. *Alternating-emphasis mesocycles.* JTS sequences hypertrophy → strength → peaking and argues against "bouncing between training styles" because "the hypertrophy phase makes the strength phase more productive with its added muscle" ([JTS](https://www.jtsstrength.com/nonsense-periodization-powerlifting/)). Moesgaard supports periodising for strength, not for hypertrophy.

**Fourth goal, or hypertrophy plus strength schemes line by line?**

The case for a fourth goal: a powerbuilding program version has defaults no other goal has. Priority lifts exist as a concept (which lines are judged on e1RM and which on reps-at-RIR), the dose target splits (per-lift sets for the priority lifts, per-muscle sets for the rest), the deload is lighter and the block ends with a test week, and the check-in asks two questions (did the top set move, did the back-off and accessory reps move). Stating all that line by line on a hypertrophy version leaves the program-level facts (priority lift set, test week, split dose rule) with nowhere to live, and the agent must infer the goal from the lines. BBM, SBS and JTS all sell it as a named thing.

The case for hypertrophy plus strength schemes: the evidence gives powerbuilding no knob of its own. Every line is either a strength line (load-progressed, RIR as fatigue cap, judged on e1RM) or a hypertrophy line (double progression, RIR as stimulus, judged on reps), and the line's scheme already says which. The dose rule is per-muscle in all three goals and counts the strength lines' hard sets at their muscles. The test week is a WeekPlan override, as program-practice.md already argues for every strength re-test. ADR 0009 took this position: "Powerbuilding is `hypertrophy` plus strength schemes on the chosen lifts, stated line by line."

The strongest resolution (opinion): keep three goal values and add one program-version fact, the **priority lifts**, a short ordered list of lines the version is judged on by e1RM. A `strength` version has priority lifts and a strength scheme on them by definition; a `hypertrophy` version with priority lifts named is powerbuilding; a `maintenance` version with priority lifts names what must hold. That carries every powerbuilding default (what to test, what goes first in a session, what the check-in asks first) without a fourth enum value or a line-by-line inference.

## 9. Rest intervals

- Trained men, 3 min vs 1 min between sets for 8 weeks: longer rest gave larger 1RM squat and bench gains and more quadriceps growth (evidence, [Schoenfeld 2016 JSCR](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26605807)).
- Strength review: "rests longer than two minutes seem to be needed to maximize strength gains in resistance-trained people"; 60–120 s suffices for the untrained (evidence, [Grgic 2018](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28641044)).
- Hypertrophy Bayesian meta-analysis, 9 studies: a small benefit to rest >60 s, "perhaps mediated by reductions in volume load"; no appreciable extra growth beyond ~90 s; mostly untrained participants (evidence, [Singer 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2024.1429789/text)).

So: strength lines rest 2–5 min, hypertrophy lines 1.5–3 min, with rest a property of the line's load, not a goal switch. Confidence: moderate.

## Implications for the domain model

For each knob: does the goal set a **default the author fixes once on the program version**, or a **rule the weekly loop applies**? Marked evidence (the split follows from a cited finding) or opinion (the split is a design judgement).

| Knob | Where it lives | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Rep range and intensity | **Program-version default**, per line via its scheme | Fixed for a block in every source (program-practice.md). The goal supplies the default scheme when a line names none: strength ≥80% / 1–6 reps on priority lifts, hypertrophy 6–15 at a target RIR, maintenance the same as the goal it maintains. Evidence (load specificity, sections 1 and 2). |
| RIR target | **Program-version default** on the scheme; **weekly rule** moves it within the block | The target per goal is fixed (strength 2–4 RIR as a fatigue cap, hypertrophy 1–3 as the stimulus); the block-week walk from ~4 toward ~1 under hypertrophy is the scheme applied to the hard-week count, not a stored weekly value. Evidence on the target, opinion on the walk. |
| Weekly set volume | **Program-version default** (the dose target ADR 0009 already gives) checked by a **weekly rule** | The target is a block fact; the week is warned against it. Under strength the target is per priority lift, not per muscle, because the strength dose-response is per lift (Ralston, Androulakis-Korakakis). Under hypertrophy the within-block set ramp (RP) is the scheme reading the hard-week count. Evidence on the targets, opinion on the ramp. |
| Progression model | **Program-version default**, per line | Chosen once per block in every source. The goal fixes the default when a line names none: strength → RIR-autoregulated load or a training-max percentage with top set + back-offs; hypertrophy → double progression on straight sets; maintenance → hold load, no progression step. The step itself (last exposure → this prescription) is `prescribe`, derived, never stored (ADR 0003, 0005). Opinion with evidence that autoregulation suits strength (Helms 2018, Hickmott). |
| Stall handling | **Weekly rule** raises the warning; **Decision** resolves it | The engine can detect the trend the goal cares about (e1RM flat over N exposures on a priority lift; reps-at-RIR flat across a muscle's lines) and warn. What to do is coaching judgement with a reason: training-max Decision, override, deload, swap at a block boundary. The goal changes the *signal* the rule watches, not who acts. Evidence that the signals differ; opinion on the warning codes. |
| Deload shape | **Program-version default** (block policy) applied by a **WeekPlan override** | Keep load, cut sets (hypertrophy) or cut reps / cut sets lightly (strength) is a block-policy fact; the light week itself is the typed override program-practice.md already proposes. A re-test week is a WeekPlan override available only when the version has priority lifts. Evidence that intensity is held (Delphi); opinion on the shape per goal. |
| Exercise priority and order | **Program-version default** (line order in a session; priority lifts) | Order is authored, not derived weekly. The goal's effect is that priority lifts must come first in their session (Nunes) and that a validator can warn when they do not. Evidence on order specificity. |
| Concurrent strength + hypertrophy | **Program-version fact**: `priority_lifts` on the version, no fourth goal value | Section 8. The goal stays one of three; priority lifts carry the powerbuilding defaults (test week, session order, what the check-in asks first, per-lift dose target). Opinion. |
| Rest intervals | **Line default**, set by the scheme, not the goal | Rest follows load (section 9); the goal only chooses the scheme. Evidence. |
| The 43+ re-test rule | **Not a rule** under any goal | ADR 0005 already removed it; nothing here argues it back. A strength version may *choose* a re-test WeekPlan after a layoff; the engine never schedules one. Opinion. |

**What the agent reads from the goal** (opinion, for the skills ticket): the check-in asks first about the priority lifts under strength (did the top set move, how did it feel), first about reps and pump at the target RIR under hypertrophy, and only whether the kept lifts held under maintenance. The plan-draft skill explains a prescription under strength as "e1RM × scheme", under hypertrophy as "last week's reps at RIR + the block's set ramp", under maintenance as "held". The profile prose adds the *why* (a meet date, a body-composition phase) and never the numbers.

**Tensions.**

- *Per-lift vs per-muscle dose.* ADR 0009's dose target is per muscle. Strength evidence is per lift. A version with priority lifts needs both counts, and the hard-set definition (direct 1, indirect 0.5) already matches Pelland's fractional counting, so the per-muscle count can stay the only stored target while the per-lift count is derived from the priority lifts' lines.
- *Maintenance as a goal vs maintenance as a dose.* The evidence treats maintenance as a dose cut (a third, intensity held) on an existing program, not a different program. That argues for `maintenance` keeping the inherited schemes and cutting only the dose target, which is what ADR 0009 already does. The open question is whether a maintenance version must name what it maintains (the previous version's goal), so the schemes have a source.
- *Goal on the version vs goal per line.* Everything above is carried by the line's scheme once the version's goal has supplied the default. If the glossary keeps "Emphasis fixes the default weekly dose target", it should add "and the default scheme for a line that names none".
